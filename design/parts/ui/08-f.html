What it has to be, for it to be worth anything

It never appears at the gate. The evening gate is a panel at screen-saver level that arrives over whatever is on your display — that is D18's whole design, and it is precisely why a private claim cannot be on it. Private claims settle inside private mode, when you go there. That is not a second gate; D5's one-gate rule is about the thing that interrupts you, and this never interrupts.

Nothing about it leaks into the surfaces that aren't private. Not the morning beat, not the record, not the web dashboard, not a notification, not the Counsel — unless you are inside private mode when you ask. The closed state above names no sentence and shows no count, because a count is information too.

The model context is separate. Whatever the AI knows about a private claim is not available to it when it is writing your ordinary evening line. Otherwise the app says something true and unexplainable on a shared screen, which is worse than saying nothing.

No illustration. D16 generates a per-claim engraving every 28 days and puts it on the record. A private claim gets none — or gets one that only exists inside private mode. Undecided, but it cannot be the current behaviour.

The one that is a real fork, and it is yours to call

FORCE-V2 §1b says state syncs to a server, and that was a deliberate choice — cloud sync and restore. Private claims put that under pressure: the reason to keep something private is usually not "hide it from my flatmate" alone.

Two honest options. Device-only — private claims never leave the machine, which means no sync, no restore, and if you lose the laptop you lose that record. Simple, and the promise is easy to keep. End-to-end encrypted — they sync, but the key is derived on-device and the server holds ciphertext it cannot read; restore works if you still have the key. More work, and the honest version of it means the web dashboard can never show them either, because the browser would need the key.

I would take device-only for v1 and say so plainly in the UI, because a privacy promise you can explain in one sentence is worth more than one you can only explain in a paragraph. But this is a decision with a real cost either way and it goes in the record with its reasoning.
