Underdamped, once, then still

A few percent of overshoot, one time, and at rest fast enough that you are not waiting on it. Two overshoots or a visible wobble has left D20 regardless of what the numbers say. The constants are not written here on purpose — they live in settle_scene.dart and a number copied into prose is wrong the first afternoon someone tunes it.

Why it must be a throw

This is the piece that makes the settle an act rather than a confirmation. A broken day you threw into the strip lands differently from one you placed there — and you did that, not the app.

Then the app says nothing

The mark lands and the panel goes. No closing line, no summary, no "see you tomorrow". Under Principle 3 there is nothing in the record tonight that is worth a sentence, and silence is the default output, not the fallback.

Flutter's own spring can't do this — animateWith(SpringSimulation) cannot retarget mid-flight, which is the entire reason to use a spring. Force writes its own simulation clock seeded from current position and velocity.

The exit is a statement, not a dismissal

It says Not tonight. It does not say Skip, Close, Later, Maybe tomorrow or Remind me. Those are all ways of not answering; this is an answer.

unsettled is not broken

Broken means you showed up and lost. Unsettled means you didn't show up. Conflating them corrupts the record — and unsettled is the more diagnostic of the two, because a run of unsettled days is the app dying, and that is the one thing v1 could never see about itself.

The machine is never locked

macOS offers windowShouldClose → false and .terminateCancel; the strict-mode spike verified both work. Force does not take them. A gate blocking a machine you earn money on is a hostage situation with your income attached — and v1's // the "no escape" rule produced exactly one outcome: total escape.

Why two buttons and not a microphone

This is a repair, not a beat. Re-showing last night's question builds a nag, and asking someone to speak about a day they already declined to speak about is the same question in a costume.

The two answers go different places

Bad day is a day that happened and hurt — the record keeps it and the app says nothing more. Busy day is the app's own early warning: two of those in a row and the gate is arriving at a time that doesn't fit your life, which is a problem with the schedule, not with you.
