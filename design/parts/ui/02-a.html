Read this before you like the flood

D19 gives accent one use only, and DESIGN.md closes the list in as many words: the emphasis mark, plus the ring contour while your thumb is down — “That is the complete list.” A full-window accent field is a third use and by far the largest. It is not an application of D19; it is a proposed amendment to it, and this page has no authority to make that call.

DESIGN.md already refused a smaller version of this move. A full-bleed illustration in accent is listed as not decided, on the grounds that it “would put more of that colour on one screen than the rest of the app uses in a month.” A whole window is more than that again.

The case for it is D16's generating principle — what repeats must be abstract or absent; what is rare can be vivid — and this fires about thirteen times a year. The case against is that D19's whole discipline is that the colour points at one thing. If you want it, it goes into FORCE-V2 as a numbered change to D19 with its reasoning. The flood is the ceremony

Accent grows out from under your thumb until it has taken the whole window, then drops to the ground colour as the word arrives. It is the only time in the product that colour fills a screen — which is exactly why it can only happen once per pursuit.

The sentence inverts under the colour

Two copies of the screen, pixel-aligned: the normal one, and the same words in bg clipped to exactly the circle the flood has reached. So the sentence doesn't get covered — it turns over as the colour arrives, and the boundary is the edge of the circle.

knowingly breaks D17 bg on accent measures 3.87:1, and ink on it is 4.07:1. Nothing passes 4.5:1 on that orange — not white, not black, not pure #000, which only reaches 4.30:1. So this is the one place in Force where the legibility floor is broken on purpose, for about four seconds, on a sentence you wrote and were reading a moment earlier.

If that is not acceptable, the arithmetic says what to do: lighten the flood past a relative luminance of 0.20 and dark text clears 4.5:1, or darken it below 0.144 and ink clears it. Either is a change to D19, which is the same call the flood itself needs.

Five and a half seconds, and it remembers

Long enough that you cannot do it while thinking about something else. Release and it falls back over ~2.4 s — slower than it filled, because backing out should not feel like a slap — and it never resets, so two short holds add up to one long one.

The rule gets out of the way first

The emphasis rule retracts the moment the hold starts, so the accent field and the accent mark are never on screen together. Otherwise the first half-second of every commit would be two accents at once, which is the thing D19 exists to prevent.

The numeral had to go

A 46 px 28 under the claim made the length of the pursuit the loudest thing on the screen. The commitment is the sentence; 28 is a condition attached to it, so it is one line of body text directly beneath.

Why a hold and not a button

A click is indistinguishable from dismissing a dialog. ~1.2 seconds is the smallest act that cannot be done by accident — and letting go early shrinks the circle back, so backing out is as visible as going through.

Why one hold and not three

D4 says the primary is chosen by what your mind is most willing to quietly drop — which means exactly one of the three is the one you'd lose. Committing all three with the same weight is a way of admitting you haven't picked.

No verdict, no strip, no mark

A background claim that could be settled is a second gate wearing a smaller hat. These are read, not answered. They surface again with their own evidence, and at day 7 and day 28.

“just the one for now” is a real exit

Someone whose yap produced only one honest answer should not be handed two more to carry. The screen offers, it does not fill quotas.
