B3b

Hold to speak — four other ways

D20 locks the ring — these would amend it new

All five run off the same amplitude engine — the real one, RMS to dBFS, floored at −58 dB, asymmetric attack and release at 26/s up and 8.5/s down. Only the instrument changes. Hover any panel to make it speak. The question to hold each one against: on night twenty, is this something you look at, or something you have stopped seeing?

1 · The layered ring D20

Three solid bodies, back to front. The one that exists.

2 · The rule typographic

…went anyway, didn't want to

The emphasis mark, extended. Your voice draws the line under your own words as you say them.

3 · The breath no meter at all

I am someone who trains when I don't feel like it.

Nothing new on the screen. The sentence's own weight and optical size move with your voice — the variable font is the instrument.

4 · The column history, not level

Every second you have spoken, stacked and scrolling up. You can see the shape of the whole answer, including the pauses.

5 · The well duration

The ground itself rises while you keep going, and the surface moves with your voice. Stop, and it holds where it got to.

How to judge these

Not “which looks best.” Force runs this ~340 times a year, so the only question that matters is which one you would still be glad to see on night twenty.

Every one of them is quiet on purpose. This is the screen you face at 11pm on a bad day, and it has to be the sentence and your voice — nothing else asking to be looked at.

What each one is actually saying to you

The ring says you are being taken in. It is an object that exists while you speak and stops existing when you stop. Its weakness is that it is a circle, and every voice interface on earth is a circle.

The rule says this is being written down. It is the only one that reuses a mark Force already has — the accent rule under the falsifiable clause — so it costs no new visual vocabulary. It is also the quietest, which on night twenty may be the whole argument.

The breath says the sentence is listening. Nothing is added to the screen at all; the claim you are defending is the thing that moves. It is the most distinctive option and the most fragile: it needs the variable-font axes pinned per frame, it can read as a rendering fault if overdone, and there is no affordance telling a new user their microphone is live.

The column says look how much you said. It is the only one with memory — the pauses are visible, and a four-word night looks obviously different from a ninety-second one. That is either honest feedback or quiet grading, and D3 forbids the second.

The well says keep going. It rewards duration, which is the one thing this app must never ask for. Included because it is the most seductive of the five and I think it is wrong: a screen that fills up when you talk longer teaches you to talk longer, and D3's floor is one sentence in your own words, not a word count.

Where this stands against the record

D20 locks the layered ring. Nothing here overrides that, and the gate screens in B2, B3 and F3 all still use it. These four exist because you asked to see other directions, and swapping any of them in is an amendment to D20 that goes into FORCE-V2 with its reasoning.

If it were mine to call I would take the rule, and I would keep the ring for night one only — the yap is the one moment where a big obvious "you are recording" object earns its place, and by night twenty the rule is the one you would still be happy to see. But the test is the one at the top of this plate, and you are the one who has to face it at 11pm.
