Section C

When it stops being a whisper

Nothing, then a question, then a trial (D12). Most nights the gate is one prompt and thirty seconds. It opens into a real conversation only on the five triggers in D6 — the day settled broken, the testimony was vague, Tempo contradicts you, it's a second consecutive miss, or the 28-day review is due. Every one of those is a moment where a human who cared about you would say something. On every other night it shuts up, and that is precisely what buys it the right not to.

Once means once

yeah, I know is a real exit and it works. The app drops it and closes the day. Pushing twice on the same point is nagging, and nagging is the behaviour that gets an app uninstalled at 1am.

The four lines are the argument

It doesn't say you've been a little vague lately. It prints the four days. You can't argue with your own record and you don't need the sentence explained.

Where "more" lives

not in the record D23 settles that the gate is short by default with an expandable "more"; the affordance is undecided. Here the four quoted days are the more, revealed with the push rather than behind a control. That is a proposal.

Both exits are real

Miscalibrated claim → rewrite the wording, always free (D4). Hostile week → name it, and the app genuinely backs off. If the week is wrong led to more pushing it would be a fake option, and one fake option poisons every other choice the app offers.

No third option called "I'll try harder"

That is the one answer with no consequence attached, so it is the one everybody picks and it teaches the app nothing.

Still not a trial of you

The thing on trial is a sentence. D12 puts the claim in the dock deliberately — a claim can be wrong without anybody having failed, and that distinction is the difference between a record you keep and a record you avoid.

C5

Stopping early — the receipt

D4 receiptD23 D14 native only

This screen carries the exact sentence that produced D23. It is the only place in the product where the app argues for staying, and it does it without a single verb aimed at you.

Stopping

You started this 19 days ago. You've gone on 11 of them. There are 9 days left.

Stop now and you never find out if it would have stuck.

say what the evidence showed and why you're stopping

The rejected version, kept on purpose

Drop it now and you're not deprioritising a habit — you're retiring a claim four days early, without the receipt.

Three abstractions in twenty-three words. You cannot see a deprioritisation. There is a number in it — four days early — and it does no work, because it hangs off the end of a concept you have already stopped parsing. Concreteness is not "contains a digit".

Why the shipped one works

19, 11, 9 — front-loaded, in the order you would count them, each one a fact only your record contains. Then a stake you can picture: a future you can see yourself not having. And it never tells you to stay.

You cannot do this from a keyboard

D14 makes retiring native-only, and the receipt rule does half the work on its own — exiting requires a microphone, because it requires you to say it.
