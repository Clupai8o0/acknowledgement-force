Section G

Verification

v1 shipped colours nobody had measured and the failures surfaced seven months later, in a spike, by accident. So the checks below run in your browser against the screens you just scrolled past — they read the rendered pixels rather than quoting the design doc. If a plate above drifts, the numbers here go red on their own.

G1

Contrast, measured on this page

D17WCAG 2.1 sRGB

Every row samples a real element from the screens above — its computed colour against its computed background, walked up the tree until something opaque is found.

Two rows clear their floor and would fail a different one, which is the whole reason they are marks rather than type. broken #8A7566 at 4.34:1 and accent #B4553A at 3.87:1 both pass 3:1 and both miss 4.5:1 — so any text that names a broken day is written in ink2, and the emphasised words stay ink while the accent lives in the rule beneath them. That is arithmetic on a locked palette, not a taste call.

Watch ink3 on a raised surface: 4.62:1, with 0.12 of headroom. Do not stack it on bg2 and then also reduce its opacity or dim it on selection — there is nothing left to spend. The showcase hit this exact wall, and its answer is the right one: the strip does not dim on selection at all; selection is carried by a halo and by size, which cost no contrast.

One genuine miss, and it is the one on the list below: unsettled #5A6066 at 2.97:1, which is 0.03 under the 3:1 floor.

G2

Where accent appears — counted, not claimed

D19 one use

D19 gives accent #B4553A exactly one resting use in the entire product: the emphasis mark on the falsifiable half of the claim. The moment it appears twice on one screen it stops pointing at anything. This walks every element on the page and counts what actually resolved to that value.

The page chrome — headings, rails, chips, buttons, tables, this callout — uses no accent at all. The only two places it appears outside a screen are the swatch in the token grid and the swatch in G1's table, where the colour is the subject rather than the styling. That was a constraint on building the page, not a coincidence, and it is what makes the count mean something: if the surrounding document had spent the colour on a link or a highlight, the table above would be measuring nothing.

One appearance cannot be counted from the DOM and is declared here instead: the innermost contour of the hold-to-speak ring is drawn to a canvas and borrows accent for as long as the thumb is down. That is the transient exception DESIGN.md records, and with it DESIGN.md closes the enumeration in as many words — “That is the complete list.”

And one thing the table cannot see, which is not settled

The commit flood on A3 takes the whole window in accent. The table above does not count it because it sits at scale(0) until you hold the ring and zero painted area is zero ink — but that is a measurement convention, not permission. D19 says accent has one use. A full-window field is a third use and the largest by three orders of magnitude, so it is a proposed amendment to D19, not an application of it. It is drawn on A3 with the argument on both sides, and it is in H5 as open.

This is the failure mode this whole section exists to catch, and it caught me: I built the thing, wrote it up as though the record permitted it, and had to be told. A census that its author can quietly define his way out of is not a census.

G3

The variable-font trap

DESIGN.md · the sharpest edge in the port

Fraunces' fvar default instance is wght 900 / opsz 9 — Black, at caption optical size. Flutter instantiates that default. Ask for a 30 px claim without saying otherwise and you get this, with no error, no log line and no missing-glyph box:

Unpinned — the fvar default

I am someone who trains when I don't feel like it.

wght 900 · opsz 9 · 30px

Pinned, as every TextStyle must be

I am someone who trains when I don't feel like it.

wght 430 · opsz 30 · 30px

CoreText did optical-size mapping for free on the SwiftUI build. Flutter does not, and there is nothing to turn on. A style without fontVariations is a bug, not a default — pin wght and opsz on every single one, with opsz set to the pixel size.

Both panels above are the same font file at the same 30 px. The difference is two numbers. This page pins every style the same way the Dart will have to.

G5

The four motions, isolated

D20

House curve is cubic-bezier(.16, 1, .3, 1) — fast out of the gate, long tail. Nothing in Force overshoots except the settle, which earns it. A panel that bounces open teaches you that things in Force bounce, and then the one mark a night that is supposed to land with weight lands like everything else does.

1 · Ink Bloom — 1.15s

I am someone who trains when I don't feel like it.

Whole sentence out of blur at once. No stagger: the claim is one thought, and a reveal you have already seen nineteen times is a wait.

2 · Italic + rule — 0.72s

I am someone who trains when I don't feel like it.

Begins 0.9s after the bloom settles. The gap is the point — the sentence is read whole first, then the app puts its finger on the half that can break.

3 · The layered ring

Three solid bodies, back to front. Depth from overlap and occlusion, never a gradient.

A spring, not a duration. It ends when it stops.

Measured, with the caveats stated

Over a 6.5-minute auto-drive of all six showcase scenes: 14,884 frames, zero exceptions, zero layout overflows. 22 frames (0.15%) over the 120 Hz budget, 3 frames (0.02%) over 60 Hz. Memory plateaued and then declined — 355.8 MB peak, 262.8 MB at the end, flat to within 0.3 MB over the final 90 s.

Both caveats from the spike's own notes: the iOS Simulator vsyncs at 60 Hz, so 120 Hz headroom is an inference rather than a measurement; and these are debug-mode numbers. Neither closes without a physical ProMotion device.

What is faithful here and what isn't

The bloom technique, the 0.9s gap, the ring's smoothing constants and the spring's shape are the real ones. The ring's amplitude is synthesised because a web page has no microphone, and the spring integrates in JavaScript rather than Dart.
