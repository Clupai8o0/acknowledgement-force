Force — every screen

Twenty-seven states across six acts, desktop only. The real controls work: hold the line and talk, pick a candidate, hold the ring for five and a half seconds, throw the mark. ← and → walk them in order and the six act names in the bar jump straight to one — or press 1–6. esc opens this list, N says why a screen is like it is, B opens the Motion panel, R replays an entrance, H hides all of this.

The sentence arrives alone. For the first 1.2 seconds a screen is one claim resolving out of blur on an empty surface — no eyebrow, no record, no microphone, no exit. Only when it has landed does the rest compose around it, and only after that does the app underline the half that can be wrong. This costs nothing: D20 already spends 0.9 s between the bloom settling and the rule drawing, and that gap was always empty. The screen is complete at the same moment it was before — the claim just no longer shares its entrance with the furniture.

Screens stagger; sentences never do. A record strip is twenty-eight days in sequence, a candidate list is three offers made one at a time, a week of testimony is a week — those arrive in their own order because that order is what they mean. D20 rejected per-word stagger on the claim and gave the reason, and the reason still holds: a reveal you have already seen nineteen times is a wait. The gate's seven marks sweep in 306 ms; the day-28 review's twenty-eight take 900, because you sat down for that one. No sweep runs longer than 900 ms whatever it is sweeping, and the rule waits for the whole screen rather than for a stopwatch — D20's 0.9 s gap is a minimum, not a schedule. B has a slider for all of it.

Everything before a commitment is reversible; the commitment is not. Say more, pick a different sentence, start again, say it again, take back a not tonight, undo a thrown mark before the panel goes. The one screen with no way back on it is the one where you commit to 28 days — D4 — because a product that asks for exactly one irreversible act can only ask honestly if everything around it is cheap. That rule is not in FORCE-V2 yet and it should be.

The transition between screens is not in the record. FORCE-V2 says nothing about how one surface replaces another, so this is a proposal: it travels in the direction you asked for — forward, the screen you left drifts up and out and the new one arrives from below — and it does not blur. Diffusion belongs to the Ink Bloom alone, and a page transition that also blurs spends the one moment in the product where something resolves out of nothing.
