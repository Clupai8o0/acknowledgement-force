Once · night one

You talk, it proposes, you commit. The whole act runs one time per pursuit and ends with the only thing in Force you cannot take back.

Every day · the loop

A ten-second briefing in the morning, and one gate at night — fired when you close the day, not when a clock says so.

Only on a trigger · when it stops being a whisper

Most nights the gate is one prompt and thirty seconds. It opens into a conversation on the five triggers in D6 and on nothing else. These four are independent illustrations, not one bad week.

Twice a window · the reviews

The only two surfaces the app is allowed to go long on, because they are the only two you sat down for.

Any time · you open these

Nothing here interrupts you and nothing here can settle a day. The split is structural: the web API has no endpoint that writes a verdict.

What this drawing is asking you

Four shapes here are proposals, not decisions. The other two — how background claims get created — is my reading of D4, not something FORCE-V2 says. Speaking uses the moving line, which amends D20's layered ring. Committed floods the window in accent, which amends D19. Private is not in the record at all and carries its own fork: device-only, or end-to-end encrypted.

And one rule the whole drawing rests on: everything before a commitment is reversible, the commitment is not, and the screen says which it is. Count the dashed arrows — there are six, and none of them touch the orange edge. That rule is not in FORCE-V2 either and it should be.

What is not drawn

The phone. macOS ships first and alone for 28 consecutive days (D22), so the narrow layout is a later pass. Also absent: strict mode's panel, which lives in settings and is never auto-offered (D12); and the 28-day illustration, which regenerates per claim and only appears on the reviews and the record (D16).
