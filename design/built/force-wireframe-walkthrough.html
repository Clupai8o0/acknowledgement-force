00

The model in one screen

Everything is built on one unit: a claim you've made about who you are, and the evidence each day either produces for it or doesn't. Habits aren't chores here — they're evidence.

Kept

You did it, and you said what you did. The day closes with something in it.

Broken

You showed up and lost. Spoken honestly. Worth more to the system than a kept day — it's the only kind of data that can change anything.

Unsettled

You didn't answer. Not the same as broken. A run of these means the app is dying — the one thing v1 could never see about itself.

D2 · the atomic unitA good claim names its own evidence and can be false. “I take care of my body” can absorb any day, so it can never be broken — which means it can never be kept either.
D12 · never miss twiceFirst miss: nothing happens. No colour change, no broken-streak graphic, no reminder. Second consecutive: the gate opens into conversation. Third: the claim goes on trial.

D7 · there is no close buttonThere's a button that says Not tonight. One tap, no confirm dialog, no guilt copy — it closes instantly. The moment quitting the night gets expensive, you quit the app instead. v1's App.swift:30 says “the no escape rule”. It produced total escape.
D3 · evidence is produced, not assertedA checkbox can be ticked while your mind is elsewhere — that's the entire problem. Saying one specific sentence out loud makes you go and get the day out of your memory, and that retrieval is the intervention, not the paperwork around it.
Tempo fills itself inForce never asks whether you trained. Your workout data is already connected, so it opens with the consequence instead of the question.
“I didn't” must always close the gateIf the only exit is success, you'll either lie to it or stop opening it — and v1 shows which one you pick.
D8 · 4am day boundaryA 12:30am evening beat belongs to that day, not the next one. Your shifts are chaos; the clock has to match the life or the app starts telling you you missed days you didn't.
D11 · what it's allowed to sayOnly things impossible without your record. “That's okay, keep it up” is banned — that's a talking fortune cookie and you'd tune it out faster than the contract. Silence is a valid output.

the trial · third miss D12

The claim goes on trial

Three in a row. Is this claim wrong, or is this week wrong?

Miscalibrated → you rewrite the wording, free, right now. Hostile week → it backs off instead of pushing.

escalation is earned by data, never randomThe gate opens into conversation on exactly five triggers: the day broke, the testimony was vague, Tempo contradicts you, second consecutive miss, or the 28-day review is due. Every one is a moment a human who cared about you would say something.
strict mode is never offered hereSuggesting the daytime-interruption tier right after you've failed reads as punishment, and you'd feel handled. It lives in settings, gets mentioned once at onboarding, and is never brought up again. You go looking for it on a good day.

What's deliberately missing

Things that exist in the plan but aren't drawn yet, so you know they weren't forgotten.

Not drawnWhy
ColourThe palette is the next decision. Three day-states need to be expressible, which the old monochrome system physically can't do — Theme.swift:69 maps accent, info and sale all to ink.
MotionThe settle animation is the emotional centre of the gate and deserves its own pass. The Flutter showcase is being built for exactly this.
Background claimsTwo quiet claims accumulate evidence without being interrogated. Only the primary gets the gate's attention.
Strict modeThe opt-in daytime-interruption tier. Deliberately buried in settings.
Settings, the register dial, voice pickingFour voices chosen for stance, not demographics. Possibly the same control as the register dial — that's a prototype question, not a spec one.
Writing and photographing a journalAgreed and adopted: speaking, typing, and photographing a handwritten page are all first-class ways to answer. Some people think by writing. A photo of a page you actually wrote is the strongest evidence there is. Not drawn yet.
RecallYour other app. Noted, not designed. Separate conversation.
Windows & Android layoutsFlutter renders identically across all three, so the phone frames above are the Android layout.

What I need from you

  1. Where does this feel wrong? Not "is it good" — where would you, specifically, bounce off it at 11pm on a bad night.
  2. Are the words right? The copy on these screens is the product. If a sentence sounds like an app talking, say so and I'll cut it.
  3. Is anything missing that you expected to see — a screen you assumed existed and can't find here.
  4. Is there too much? The fastest way to kill this is to ship all of it. Tell me which sections you'd defer and I'll scope v1 down to the loop.
